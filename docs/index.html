How does this work?

This dashboard fetches live data from four federal API endpoints and blends three independent forecast methods:

Data Sources

  1. Recent streamflow — 90 days of daily mean discharge from USGS NWIS
  2. Historical statistics — Percentiles (p10–p90) for each day-of-year from the USGS Statistics Service (decades of record)
  3. Historical analogs — Same-time-of-year flow data from 15 past years for KNN pattern matching
  4. Weather forecast — 7-day gridded temperature & precipitation from the NWS API

Forecast Methods

  1. AR(2) Persistence — Second-order autoregressive model in log-space with momentum capture. Uses two lagged terms (Yule-Walker estimation) for better short-term accuracy. Includes flow-dependent φ (anomalous flows decay faster) and recession curve fitting on falling limbs.
  2. Trend Extrapolation — Log-space linear trend from the last 7 days, extrapolated forward with dampening (fades to zero by day 5). Captures short-term momentum that AR models smooth away.
  3. KNN Historical Analogs — Finds the 7 most similar historical years from 15 candidates using a 4-feature distance metric (flow level, trajectory, acceleration, variability). Blended via log-space weighted geometric mean with outlier trimming.
  4. Percentile-Anchored Climatology — Targets the historical percentile matching the current flow position, gradually reverting toward the median (p50) over the forecast horizon.

Blending & Uncertainty

  • All blending performed in log-space (geometric means) to prevent high-flow bias
  • Short-term (day 1–3): ~25% Trend + ~40% AR(2) + ~25% KNN + ~10% climatology
  • Medium-term (day 4–7): ~25% AR(2) + ~25% KNN + ~40% climatology
  • Long-term (day 8–14): ~10% AR(2) + ~20% KNN + ~60% climatology
  • Adaptive weights: dynamic skill-based weighting from recent retrocast performance; anomalous flows reduce climatology weight
  • Bias correction: retrospective AR(2) error analysis corrects systematic over/underprediction (clamped ±15%)
  • Season-aware NWS temperature perturbation (highest sensitivity in spring snowmelt, lowest in winter baseflow)
  • Uncertainty bands from KNN trace spread (short-term) blended with historical p10/p90 percentiles (long-term)

The dashed yellow lines on the forecast chart show the historical p25/p50/p75 envelope — where this river has typically flowed at this time of year.
